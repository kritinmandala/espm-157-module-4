Arctic sea ice and atmospheric CO₂

September is when Arctic sea ice reaches its yearly minimum. This app puts that September extent next to the year's average atmospheric CO₂ at Mauna Loa, Hawaii. Pick a year to see the values, the 1979–2025 record, and the ice on a polar map.

About the data

The app compares two measured records. The CO₂ series is the annual mean of the monthly mean CO₂ at Mauna Loa. The sea ice series is the September monthly mean extent in the Arctic, the month of the annual minimum. Both are shown in SI and scientific units used by the source agencies.

  • CO₂: NOAA Global Monitoring Laboratory, Mauna Loa monthly CO₂. The file is read from NOAA by the browser each time the page loads. The annual value is the mean of the 12 monthly values in the file; years with fewer than 12 months are not shown. Negative values in the file's statistics columns mark missing or interpolated months and are not treated as measurements.
  • Sea ice: National Snow and Ice Data Center, Sea Ice Index, Version 4 (G02135), Arctic monthly extent. The value is the September extent from the monthly file. Values of -9999 mark missing months and are treated as missing. 2025 uses the NSIDC-0803 processing stream; earlier years use NSIDC-0051.
  • Years: 1979–2025. The sea ice record starts in 1979, and 2026 is left out because the CO₂ year is not complete (the CO₂ file runs through September 2026). A complete year is one with all 12 monthly CO₂ values and a September sea ice value.
  • Map: September extent polygons and ice-edge lines from the NSIDC monthly shapefiles, projected to EPSG:3411. NSIDC's download server does not send CORS headers, so the browser cannot fetch those files directly. scripts/build_data.py downloads the original CSV and shapefiles and writes the copies in web/data/. Small isolated grid cells more than 4,000 km from the pole are dropped from the map; they are source artifacts, not Arctic ice. Land comes from Natural Earth 1:110m.
  • Interpretation: the scatter and trend line describe an association between two time series, not a causal claim. Many other factors affect sea ice.
